> ## Design: Engin as the online hiring hackathon for early-stage startups
> 
> Generated by /office-hours on 2026-09-29
> Branch: main
> Repo: roshan-san/engin-2026
> Status: APPROVED

> 
> ### Problem Statement
> 
> Early-stage founders in India hire their first 1-5 builders through their network and LinkedIn/X DMs, then make a gut call after a chat. Nothing shows whether the person can actually ship. Builders without pedigree can't get past that filter, because there's no verified record of what they've shipped.
> 
> Engin's answer: a founder runs a short **online hiring hackathon** (a Trial Cycle). Each contributor gets their own board seeded with the founder's challenges, messages the founder in one channel, and ships. The founder gives a verdict, sends the winner an offer, and the result lands on the contributor's public Score.
> 
> ### Demand Evidence
> 
> None yet, as stated in the session: "No evidence yet". The strongest available signal is the founders' own need. Roshan (technical co-founder) and Dinesh (founder) need builders for Engin itself. That's a sample of one, filtered through already believing in the solution. Willingness to pay is also untested (see Assignment step 3).
> 
> ### Status Quo
> 
> Network + LinkedIn DMs + a chat + a gut call. It's free, fast and trust-based, and it's hard to beat when it works. The cost of it failing (network runs dry, or a bad hire discovered months later) has **not been measured**. That's open question #1.
> 
> ### Target User & Narrowest Wedge
> 
> - **Paying user:** a founder of a pre-seed Indian startup hiring 1 builder, often for equity.
> - **Free user:** a builder, often a student from a college E-cell, who needs verified proof of work to get into startups.
> - **Wedge:** one online hiring hackathon for one hire, with founders paying per hackathon.
> - **Incubators and E-cells are a marketing channel only.** You run sessions there and hand out launch codes. They never use Engin, and nothing is built for them.
> - **Investors are the later payoff.** No investor features, auto investor updates included, until **25 startups have each run at least one real Cycle**. This is the single threshold used throughout the doc.
> 
> ### Constraints
> 
> - Solo technical co-founder building (Roshan). A small burn (~₹60k per quarter for the team stipend).
> - The stack is already built: React + Convex, with Trial Cycles, challenges, trial messages, verdicts, offers, Score, the pitch page, Explore, and internal Cycles/Pulses.
> - Existing caps: at most 10 Participants per Trial Cycle (`MAX_TRIAL_PARTICIPANTS`, `convex/lib/limits.ts:1`) and 50 applications (`MAX_TRIAL_APPLICATIONS`).
> - Contributors are unpaid during hackathons. Competitors (Joinstartup) pay ₹10k+ per trial.
> - Equity agreements carry legal complexity, so they're deferred.
> 
> ### Premises
> 
> 1. For the next 90 days Engin is one product: the online hiring hackathon (Trial Cycle). The tracker, pitch page and Explore exist to support it.
> 2. The investor side is frozen until 25 startups have each run at least one real Cycle.
> 3. Engin's edge over Joinstartup, Thodakkam and WorkTrial is that the hackathon is **competitive and many-to-one**, graded on real Pulses in the startup's own workspace, and feeds a public Score that shows who issued each verdict. "We also do trials" is not an edge.
> 4. Success is measured in hackathons completed, hires made and founders paying, not signups.
> 5. Test zero is the founders hiring Engin's own next builder through an Engin Trial Cycle.
> 6. Pricing: **founders pay; contributors are always free; incubators are free because they're a channel, not users.** This follows the pattern at Unstop, where companies pay and students use it free.
> 
> ### Landscape (web search, 2026-09-29)
> 
> These figures are secondary-source numbers from a single search session. They're indicative, not verified.
> 
> - **Joinstartup** (India): paid Starter Projects from ₹10k, and 1-2 week paid Work Trials. The closest competitor. https://joinstartup.app/
> - **Thodakkam** (India): verified student talent and proof of work for startup internships. https://thodakkam.com/
> - **WorkTrial AI:** work simulations instead of resumes. https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/work-trial-ai
> - **Unstop:** ~$5.3M raised over 3 rounds, ₹33.1 Cr revenue in FY25 (Inc42). Hackathons and hiring challenges, mostly for corporates hiring freshers. https://inc42.com/company/unstop/
> - **Linear:** ~$134M total raised; $82M Series C at a $1.25B valuation in June 2025 (TechCrunch). https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/10/atlassian-rival-linear-raises-82m-at-1-25b-valuation/
> - **EquityList:** ₹3.0 Cr revenue in FY25, $2.2M seed (Inc42). https://inc42.com/company/equitylist/
> - **DocSend:** acquired by Dropbox for $165M in 2021. https://www.ellty.com/blog/docsend-for-startups
> - **Visible.vc:** $59/mo plan, ~$5.3M raised. https://www.ellty.com/blog/visible-pricing
> 
> This search surfaced no platform running competitive, many-to-one hackathons for an early-stage startup's single hire. That's absence in one search, not proof nobody does it.
> 
> ### Approaches Considered
> 
> - **A: Online hiring hackathon on existing code, hybrid pricing (Pro subscription with hackathon credits, or pay per hackathon).** Chosen.
> - **B: Hackathon + equity kit together.** Rejected for now: "it will come with more legal complications". It's the likely next build after the first real hire.
> - **C: Hackathon + auto investor updates.** Deferred until the 25-startup threshold.
> - **Incubator-paid cohort view.** Rejected: incubators are a channel, not users.
> - **Events as a first-class object for organizers.** Rejected: there are no organizer users.
> 
> ### Recommended Approach
> 
> **A. Make the Trial Cycle a public online hiring hackathon and charge founders through hybrid pricing.** The hackathon mechanics already exist. The new work is payments, a few rule changes, and presentation.
> 
> #### What sells (ranked)
> 1. Hiring hackathon (Trial Cycle): the paid product.
> 2. Contributor Score + public profile: the supply magnet, always free.
> 3. Public pitch page + Explore: free acquisition, and where contributors find hackathons.
> 4. Internal Cycles/Pulses: retention after the hire.
> 5. Inbox, palette, shortcuts, mobile: polish.
> 
> #### Pricing (hybrid, confirmed 2026-09-30)
> 
> | | **Free** | **Pro: ₹999/mo or ₹9,999/yr** |
> |---|---|---|
> | Pitch page, Explore listing, team workspace (Cycles/Pulses) | ✅ | ✅ |
> | Team members | 5 | 50 |
> | Stealth mode | ❌ | ✅ (but a stealth startup can't publish a public hackathon) |
> | Hiring hackathons | ₹2,999 each | 1 credit per month included; unused credits roll over, max 3 banked |
> | Extra hackathons beyond credits | ₹2,999 | ₹1,499 |
> | Contributors | always free | always free |
> 
> Pro at ₹999 costs less than one pay-as-you-go hackathon, so any founder hiring more than once a quarter should subscribe. Banked credits are the reason not to cancel between hires. If Pro is cancelled, banked credits are lost at period end, and published hackathons keep running.
> 
> #### Pricing rules (proposed, confirm)
> - **Default:** every hackathon needs a Pro credit or a one-time payment (₹2,999 on Free, ₹1,499 on Pro).
> - **Free allowance:** launch codes only. Each code gives one free hackathon to one founder account and is redeemed once. **Budget: at most 10 codes in the 90 days, and at most 2 per E-cell session.** Codes are created by an internal mutation into a `launchCodes` table (code, issuedTo note, redeemedBy, redeemedAt), and Dinesh hands them out. Engin's *first* hackathon runs on today's ungated flow (see Assignment). Later Engin hackathons use a code. So a founder's first hackathon is not free by default, and the payment criterion can be reached without repeat hackathons.
> - **Charge point:** when the founder publishes the Trial Cycle, before it opens for applications. Publish consumes one unit, in this order: launch code, then banked Pro credit, then a one-time payment. Trial Cycles gain a `draft` status. `create` inserts a draft, and the start scheduler moves from create to publish, so drafts are hidden from Explore, opportunities and `joinTrial`. An unpaid or failed payment leaves it as a draft, nothing goes public, and there's no partial state. If payment completes after the draft's application deadline or `startsAt` has passed, publish is blocked until the founder picks new dates.
> - **Refunds:** none, with one exception, granted manually by Engin rather than automatically. If a published hackathon receives fewer than 3 **applications** by its entry cutoff (`applicationDeadline`, or `startsAt` when there's no deadline), Engin grants one re-run credit on request. The trigger counts applications, not joins, so a founder can't produce it by rejecting applicants. A credited re-run can't earn another credit, and credits expire after 60 days.
> - **UPI pre-payments (before payment code ships):** a founder who pre-pays by UPI in Assignment step 3 receives a launch code for one hackathon, counts as a paying founder, and gets a full refund if Engin can't run their hackathon within 30 days. These codes don't count against the 10-code free budget.
> - **Founder Pro subscription:** kept, at ₹999/mo or ₹9,999/yr through the existing Dodo subscription. `PLAN_LIMITS` stays two-tier for members, capacity and stealth. `liveTrialCycles` is no longer a plan limit: publishing is gated by credits or payment instead (Pro's current unlimited hackathons go away). Monthly credit grant happens on the Dodo renewal webhook (`onSubscriptionActive` / `onSubscriptionRenewed` in `convex/http.ts`), capped at 3 banked.
> - **Existing subscriptions:** pre-product, so none are expected. Check the Dodo dashboard. Any existing founder Pro subscriber moves to the new Pro and starts with 1 credit.
> - **Contributor Pro:** retired. It grants nothing. Only founders buy Pro.
> - **Contributor cap:** at most 5 live entries per contributor, counting the existing `LIVE_ENTRY_STATUSES` (`applied`, `joined`), with no Pro bypass. It replaces the current 3-entry cap with Pro bypass (`convex/lib/hiring/entries.ts:45-52`). Message when hit: "You're in 5 hackathons already. Finish or withdraw from one to join another." Nothing else limits parallel entries.
> 
> #### Hackathon rules (proposed, confirm)
> - **IP of submissions:** contributors keep ownership of what they submit. A founder may use a submission only from the contributor they make an offer to (after it's accepted) or pay separately. Both sides must tick an acknowledgment at entry: founders in the publish flow, contributors in the join/apply flow. The tick is stored with a timestamp on the Trial Cycle and on the application. Founders are told to seed scoped challenges, not production-critical work.
> - **Stealth:** a startup in stealth can't publish a public hackathon. The publish flow asks the founder to turn stealth off first. So every hackathon page and every verdict names the real startup.
> - **Prize:** an optional free-text field entered by the founder (e.g. "₹5,000 to the winner"), paid off-platform. Engin doesn't handle prize money.
> - **Score integrity:** a founder can't issue a Score-bearing verdict to anyone who is already a member of their startup. Each verdict on a public profile shows the startup that issued it. Engin's own hackathon verdicts count, and are shown as issued by Engin. Until this rule ships, marketing says "founder-verified", not "verified".
>
